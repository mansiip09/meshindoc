CHALLENGES IN HIGHER EDUCATION

The Attendance Problem

Conventional attendance mechanisms fail across universities because they capture a single momentary timestamp rather than verifying continuous physical learning engagement.

PROBLEM 01
⏱️

Manual Attendance

Professors spend 15–20 minutes calling roll numbers out of a 50-minute lecture, draining hundreds of productive instructional hours annually across every academic department.

Impact: Up to 25% of total classroom teaching time lost.
PROBLEM 02
👥

Proxy & Fake Attendance

Friends call out roll numbers or pass mobile phones and RFID identity cards to bypass paper registers, mobile QR scans, and one-off GPS geofence checks.

Impact: Fabricated records with zero physical presence proof.
PROBLEM 03
🚶

No Continuous Presence Tracking

Students answer the roll call at 09:00 AM and slip out of the lecture hall at 09:12 AM. Existing biometric and manual solutions possess zero awareness of mid-lecture truancy.

Impact: Massive ghost attendance despite empty desks.
PROBLEM 04
📡

Internet Dependency

Thick concrete classroom walls, remote college locations, and frequent campus internet blackouts crash Wi-Fi and cloud biometric apps that require continuous live internet.

Impact: Systems fail completely during frequent network outages.
PROBLEM 05
👨‍🏫

Teacher Presence Uncertainty

Current software monitors students while ignoring faculty attendance. There is no automated record verifying whether the instructor arrived on time, was substituted, or left early.

Impact: Zero institutional accountability for scheduled classes.
PROBLEM 06
📁

Limited Real-Time Visibility

Attendance remains locked away in handwritten paper registers until semester exam eligibility checks, making timely interventions for struggling students impossible.

Impact: Parents and district officers receive alerts months too late.

THE PROPOSED INNOVATION

Meet MeshAttend

MeshAttend transforms passive attendance into an autonomous, continuous classroom presence intelligence network using low-power BLE signals, peer-to-peer mesh hops, and edge verification.

USP 01 // IDENTITY + PRESENCE

Identity-Linked Presence

"Student identity and physical room presence are verified together cryptographically."

Eliminates buddy-punching and proxy calls. An enrolled student ID is bound to a rolling cryptographic BLE signature that must physically radiate within the classroom boundary.

  • Zero possibility of phone-forwarding proxy
  • Mutual cross-check with instructor's beacon
  • Passive student operation without battery drain
USP 02 // DWELL MONITORING

Continuous Lecture Tracking

"Presence is monitored throughout the lecture instead of only at entry roll call."

Autonomous 3-to-5 minute background sweeps monitor student continuity. If a student slips out 20 minutes into class, the system records partial dwell time and marks the session accordingly.

  • Detects midway departures instantly
  • 75% minimum dwell threshold policy
  • Anomaly flags for suspicious RSSI fluctuations
USP 03 // OFFLINE MESH

Mesh Connectivity

"Classroom data reaches the system even when internet conditions are completely unavailable."

Individual classroom ESP32 nodes link into a Bluetooth Mesh network, hopping packets across corridors to a single campus gateway without needing Wi-Fi or cellular routers in each room.

  • Requires zero internet inside classrooms
  • Up to 30 days of offline local flash queue
  • One internet uplink covers entire academic block

MATHEMATICAL SIGNAL INTEGRITY

Understanding RSSI Proximity & Physical Limitations

Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) measures received RF signal power in decibels relative to one milliwatt (dBm). While proportional to distance via the Log-Distance Path Loss model, raw RSSI is notoriously noisy due to multipath reflection from chalkboards, metal benches, and moving bodies.

RSSI(d) = RSSI(d₀) - 10 · n · log₁₀(d / d₀) + X_σ

Key Architectural Decision: MeshAttend does not claim unrealistic centimeter-grade indoor positioning. Instead, it applies a recursive 1D Kalman filter coupled with temporal time-windowing to classify whether a device is reliably within the classroom perimeter (> -75 dBm smoothed) versus out in the hallway (< -85 dBm), preventing false-positive proxy logs.

PROACTIVE RISK MITIGATION

Risks We Designed For

Directly addressing operational, human, and governmental hurdles identified during college field research with concrete architectural countermeasures.

RISK 01

Teacher Resistance

Faculty may initially perceive classroom presence monitoring as intrusive administrative surveillance or lack of trust in their teaching autonomy.

MITIGATION STRATEGY

Protection, Not Surveillance

Position the system as an automated assistant that saves 20 minutes of tedious manual roll calls every lecture and legally defends instructors with proof of conducted classes.

RISK 02

Hardware Damage or Theft

Understaffed or rural college classrooms face risks of exposed nodes being tampered with, accidentally damaged, or stolen from walls.

MITIGATION STRATEGY

Low-Cost Replaceable Nodes

ESP32 boards cost only ₹350 and mount inside tamper-resistant junction boxes high on walls. Neighboring mesh nodes send immediate offline alerts if a device stops heartbeating.

RISK 03

Budget & Procurement Delays

State-level public university rollouts often stall for months awaiting formal tenders, financial sanctions, and centralized approvals.

MITIGATION STRATEGY

Start With Pilot Colleges

Deploy in 2–3 pilot colleges first using minor departmental contingency funds. Demonstrate demonstrable gains in instructional hours and attendance, unlocking state budget grants.
